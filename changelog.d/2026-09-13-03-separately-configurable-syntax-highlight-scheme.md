- **Separately configurable syntax-highlight scheme, decoupled from the app theme** (this session, 2026-09-13, implements FEATURE_BACKLOG.md #67). The eight `SYNTAX_COLOR_KEYS` (the embedded-XML/JSON highlight colors in the entry detail, `#detailFormatToggle`) can now be chosen independently of the app theme via a new **Syntax highlighting** picker in Settings → Appearance (`#settingsSyntaxSchemeSelect`). Its default, **"Follow app theme"** (`FOLLOW_THEME_SYNTAX_ID`), reproduces the pre-#67 behaviour exactly — `applySyntaxScheme()` is a no-op, so the `--syntax-*` vars keep coming from the active theme's own optional `syntaxColors` block (custom themes included) or the `:root` / `:root[data-theme="light"]` cascade defaults. Six **built-in syntax schemes** (`BUILTIN_SYNTAX_SCHEMES`), one curated pendant per built-in app theme — Dark/Light reproduce the `:root` defaults, the four Catppuccin flavors get palettes drawn from each flavor's own named colors — are selectable regardless of which app theme is active. **Custom syntax schemes** use our own small JSON format (`format:"philogg-syntax-scheme"`, a `name` + the 8 `colors` keys) with a "Download template…"/"+ Import scheme…" card in Settings mirroring the custom-theme UX; imports persist to `localStorage["philogg-custom-syntax-schemes"]`, the chosen scheme to `localStorage["philogg-syntax-scheme"]` (same tier as the theme/accent choices). `applyTheme()` calls `applySyntaxScheme()` after the theme's own colors are set, so a chosen scheme overrides `--syntax-*` inline on top of any theme; returning to "Follow app theme" (or deleting the active custom scheme) clears the override. A stale/deleted scheme id in storage falls back to follow-theme on init (`resolveSyntaxSchemeId`).
